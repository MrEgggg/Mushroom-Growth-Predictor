Key findings

  • CO2 is the strongest signal. The fitted equation associates an extra 100 ppm with about fewer BE points, holding its other inputs fixed; this does not establish a causal effect. Trials at 750 ppm or more averaged around 50% BE, against 80% or better below that.
  • Summer records had lower yields. Summer trials averaged about 53% BE against about 81% in the other seasons, a drop of roughly 28 points.
  • Substrate groups differed. Blue Oyster on hardwood sawdust reached about 100% BE in winter, against about 65% on supplemented sawdust.
  • Pink Oyster on wheat straw had lower winter yields. In winter (about 57°F) it fell to 35 to 37% BE, against about 76% in fall.

The error band is one residual standard deviation around the estimate, clipped to the model bounds. It is descriptive and is not a validated prediction interval. Colonization time is an observed group average, not a prediction from entered conditions.

Worked example

Blue Oyster on hardwood sawdust at 51°F and 470 ppm CO2: 105.09 + 1.4905 × 51 − 0.18789 × 470 ≈ 92.8% BE, so about 928 g from 1,000 g of dry substrate. The real trial came in at 100%.

Limitations

  • Temperature and CO2 move together in this data (correlation 0.93), so their separate effects can't be pulled apart. The positive temperature term is a correction on top of CO2, not evidence that warmer is better.
  • Small groups. Several species and substrate pairs have only 4 to 6 trials.
  • Observational data. The trials were not randomized, so the model shows association, not proven cause.
  • Tested range only: .
  • Known miss: Blue Oyster on supplemented sawdust in winter is over-predicted by 10 to 14 points (about 64% observed against about 77% predicted), and Pink Oyster on wheat straw in winter by about 6. Both suggest a species-specific temperature effect the simple model leaves out.
